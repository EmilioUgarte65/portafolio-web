In plain words

What it is and why it matters

What it is

A platformer with a building site inside

You jump, climb and dodge enemies through a building under construction while collecting materials and money. It is designed for a short session of a few minutes, and also for relaxed play on the web.

The problem

Making a catalog worth touching

Construction materials and finishes are hard to make memorable. Someone with a few minutes to spare won't download an app or wait for something heavy to load.

The solution

Play to design a space

The platformer is how you earn the materials. Money opens packs of finishes (floors, paints, wall designs) and you complete a house you can show off and compare.

Step by step

How a session works

Open a link

It starts from a link or a QR code and you play as a guest; an account is optional.

Run the level

Jump, climb ladders and scaffolding, dodge the buckets and collect materials in building order: gravel, rebar and cement.

Collect the reward

At the end, a prize wheel multiplies the reward. Money opens packs of finishes and album cards.

Finish your house

Place the materials stage by stage and decorate with finishes; publish it and compare it on the leaderboard.

Gameplay & dynamics

End-to-end game mechanics

Every system is written and tested on its own, from the level generator to the game economy.

Procedural generation

Levels that build themselves

Each level is a cross-section of a building under construction: drainage, foundations, floors, structure and roof. It is generated from a seed, so it is always different yet reproducible, and a validator checks it can be finished or tries again.

Building order

The construction recipe

The game follows the real order of construction: putting cement where rebar goes is rejected and costs the bonus. A clean pour doubles the materials.

Collection

Cards and packs

An album of collectible cards and packs with rarities. Duplicate cards are spent on painting the house, laying floors or building the garden.

Community

Other players' houses and leaderboard

Browse other players' houses and scores, leave likes and publish your own. Every account has a unique nickname; guests can only watch.

Accounts

Frictionless saving

Play as a guest or sign up with email and password or a magic link (a sign-in link, no password). Creating an account keeps your progress.

Mobile first

Built for thumbs

Multi-touch controls with enlarged touch areas. When the phone locks or you switch apps, the game pauses and saves on its own.

Engineering

WebGL optimization for play-from-a-link

When a game is shared by link, loading time decides whether people stay to play.

Unity 6 with 2D rendering (URP)

URP is Unity's lightweight render pipeline. Core logic (generator, recipe, prize wheel) lives in plain C# classes with no engine dependencies, so it can be tested on its own.

Generator identical to its prototype

The generator was first designed in Python and ported to C#. A fingerprint test (FNV, a fast checksum) proves both produce the same level, bit for bit.

EditMode and PlayMode tests

700+ tests: some validate rules and the generator across a thousand levels; others boot the game and check the player spawns, never falls through the floor and stays in frame.

Brotli compression and deferred loading

The build is served Brotli-compressed (a highly efficient compression format) with custom headers. Textures are reduced only for web: mobile start-up dropped from 25.4 to 19.6 MiB.

PWA and saves that don't get lost

A service worker (the part that stores the game on the phone) speeds up launches. Progress is saved as JSON and to IndexedDB (the browser's database), including when the tab is hidden.

Server-validated leaderboard

Accounts and leaderboard run on Supabase (a cloud Postgres database with authentication). Scores only go through RPC functions (server procedure calls) that enforce caps and never let a record go down.
