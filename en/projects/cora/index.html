In plain words

What is it and what is it for?

What is a conversational assistant?

A program that chats the way a person would. Under the hood it uses a large language model (LLM, the technology behind ChatGPT or Claude) that understands what the customer writes and decides which tool to use: look up a product, check a price or build a quote.

The problem

A retail chain with thousands of products gets WhatsApp questions at all hours. Answering price and stock by hand is slow, the price depends on who is asking and, after hours, many conversations go unanswered.

The solution

CORA answers right away with real data from the ERP (the company's sales and inventory system): it recognizes the customer by phone number, applies their price list, checks stock and closes with a PDF quote. Anything that needs human judgment goes to the right team.

How it is used

How CORA handles a message

The customer just writes on WhatsApp; everything else happens in seconds, with nobody stepping in.

Listens to the whole thing

Waits a few seconds in case the customer keeps typing and merges their messages. Voice notes are transcribed; photos are analyzed.

Recognizes the customer

Looks up the phone number in the ERP and pulls their name, tax ID (RFC) and price list without asking anything.

Uses its tools

The model decides what to do: find the product, check price and stock, add to cart or quote. It loops until it has the answer, with a cap on rounds.

Replies like a person

Sends the answer in parts, with "typing…" and natural pauses. When the case calls for it, it escalates to someone on the team.

Capabilities

Everything CORA can do

One assistant that covers the customer conversation from start to finish.

Natural conversation

Talks in everyday language, understands the intent behind each message and keeps the thread without sounding robotic. Each agent has clear rules on tone, pricing and when not to promise something.

Understands voice notes

Transcribes audio with Whisper (a speech-to-text model) and answers as if it had listened.

Reads images

Interprets photos of products or of a problem on site to give a better recommendation.

Typo-tolerant search

Finds products by code, keyword, synonyms or by how the word sounds, even when misspelled. It also searches by meaning with embeddings (numeric representations of text).

The right price

Applies the price that matches the customer's tax ID and price group, and checks stock at their preferred store.

PDF quotes

Generates the quote in the official format, with folio number, QR code and payment link, and records it in the ERP.

Proactive follow-up

Schedules reminders and reactivations during business hours, and honors a block list for anyone who does not want messages.

Hands off to a human when needed

Picks one of 14 reasons (credit, invoice, complaint, outside delivery area…) and each reason reaches the team that should handle it. When a person takes over the chat from the CRM (the system where the team sees and serves customers), CORA goes quiet; the pause expires on its own and, when the case is closed, CORA resumes.

Memory across conversations

Keeps summaries of previous chats to pick up where the customer left off, without asking them to repeat everything. Idle sessions are cleaned up automatically.

Specialized agents

A team of agents behind a single voice

CORA is the general agent, but five situations have their own agent, with its own prompt (the model's instructions) and its own set of tools.

Returns, invoicing, complaints, delivery and credit

Each agent knows its policy and only has the tools for its topic. The complaints agent cannot quote because it does not have that tool: a constraint that enforces itself beats a well-written instruction.

Keyword routing, no extra cost

The agent is chosen from the words in the message, without another model call: asking the LLM what each message is about would cost time and money on every message.

Continuity across turns

The agent that takes a conversation keeps it while it is active, so the customer never has to repeat their case.

Same-turn handoff

If an invoicing chat turns into a return, the agent passes the conversation and the receiving agent answers in the same reply. A cap prevents ping-pong loops.

Invisible to the customer

For the customer it is one conversation with one person: no agent introduces itself again or says "transferring you".

Management agent

Only for authorized phone numbers: answers sales, margins and comparisons with ERP figures, and sends the Excel or PDF on request. For any other number, that agent does not exist.

Under the hood

How it is built

A solid, tested foundation ready to grow.

Node.js and WhatsApp

Node.js server with Express. It connects to WhatsApp through whatsapp-web.js or the official API (application programming interface) via a provider that reports every message with a webhook (an automatic call from one system to another).

LLM with tools

Function calling: the model asks to run system functions and the code executes them. The AI provider (OpenAI, Claude or Gemini) is chosen at startup.

SQL Server and ERP

Its own database for history, carts and cases, plus read access to the ERP through stored procedures. The only thing it writes to the ERP is the quote.

PDFs and scheduled jobs

Quotes are rendered with Puppeteer (an automated browser) from an HTML template. node-cron jobs refresh stock, send follow-ups and schedule reactivations; PM2 keeps the process alive.

Predictive models

Logistic regression models, validated forward in time (tested on data later than the training data), estimate which quotes will close and which customers are at risk. They are recomputed every night.

Secure and tested

Credentials live in environment variables, outside the code. A test mode redirects every outgoing message to a test phone, and automated conversation suites check search, addresses, agents and flows.
