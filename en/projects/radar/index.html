Radar · Commercial analytics

Which customer to serve today, and why.

Radar is a commercial analytics portal for a retail chain. It takes the ERP history (the sales and inventory system) in a single read-only pass, stores it in a local database and answers in milliseconds: which customers should already have bought again, how much profit each one brings, which lever to use to win them back and which quotes to recover. Every priority comes with its formula in view.

In plain words

What is it and what is it for?

What is commercial analytics?

Using sales history to decide whom to call, what to offer and where the money is, instead of guessing or calling whoever comes to mind.

The problem

The list of customers and quotes is huge, with no clear rule for what to review first. On top of that, a dashboard that queries the ERP (the sales and inventory system) on every click is slow and puts load on the system operations depend on.

The solution

Radar computes each customer's own buying rhythm, how overdue they are and how much margin they bring, and ranks the queue with a transparent rule. Search, filters and paging run locally, over the whole base.

Step by step

Working with Radar

Open "Today"

See how many customers should already have bought again, overdue quotes, and where sales and profit are heading month by month.

Review the queue

Filter by segment or store, search by name or ID and read the reason behind each priority, with the formula in view.

Open the profile

Purchase history, categories and the suggested lever to win the customer back: settle balance, installments, discount or bundle.

Reach out and measure

Log channel, lever and outcome, and Radar checks whether they bought afterwards. The full selection exports to CSV (a file Excel opens).

Modules

One board to operate, another to analyze

Navigation is split into operations (whom to serve today), analysis (where the money is) and system (whether the data can be trusted).

Today

The daily summary: buying customers, repeat customers, customers to contact because their estimated date arrived, overdue quotes, and the sales and profit trend.

Customers

Prioritized customers and their purchase frequency, with profile and paginated history.

Quotes

Daily work

Products and affinity

What gets bought together, to build project bundles.

Stores

Recovery

One-time buyers and customers who are spending less, with their reactivation lever.

Promotions

Growth

Data and model

Is the data arriving? Quality indicators with explicit usage blocks and the gap between the calendar clock and the last sales cutoff.

Privacy at the query level

If a customer asked not to be contacted, their phone number is nulled in the database query itself: the data never leaves the server. The log stores only a hashed fingerprint of the customer ID.

Explainable priority

A rule you can read and debate

No black-box model: every number in the queue is explained by a formula the interface shows next to the table.

Relative delay

Delay = days without buying ÷ the customer's own historical cadence. Each customer is compared with themselves, not with an overall average.

Four windows

On time (delay ≤ 1.5) weighs 1.00; overdue (≤ 3) 0.65; at risk (≤ 5) 0.35; likely lost (> 5) 0.10. A customer ten times overdue drops in priority instead of tying with one just overdue.

Customer value

Value = 0.5 + 0.5 × historical sales percentile. Priority = 100 × window × value.

Margin, not revenue

The ranking uses profit: two customers with the same invoice can leave very different margins. Expected profit is also weighted by the likelihood they return.

The right lever

First whatever blocks an offer (overdue balance), then what costs no margin (installments), and only then a discount, if margin remains above the set floor.

Honest measurement

The contact log measures timing coincidence, not causation, and says so next to the number. Measuring causation needs a control group.

Under the hood

One pass over the ERP, everything else local

Local snapshot in SQLite

A single grouped query to the ERP (SQL Server) builds every customer's profile. It is stored in SQLite, a single-file database, with indexes and FTS5, its full-text search engine that matches prefixes and ignores accents.

A math shortcut

Average purchase cadence is (last − first) ÷ (days − 1), because the sum of consecutive intervals telescopes. That identity removes the most expensive operation in the query.

Guaranteed read-only

The adapter only accepts predefined operations: everything is SELECT, with typed parameters and a lock timeout so the ERP is never blocked. A test checks the real SQL.

Atomic rebuilds

The new snapshot is built separately and swapped in within a transaction: nobody sees a half-built snapshot, and an empty extraction keeps the previous one.

Fast under load

Per-operation cache with a time to live, a single query for identical simultaneous requests and a semaphore that limits concurrent queries.

Dependency-free Python

Python server using only the standard library, bound to the local machine. Opening it to the network requires a token, and it refuses to start without one. The public demo keeps the original interface with fictional in-memory data.
